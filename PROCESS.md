# Process overview

## What I built

An interactive circle-of-fifths explainer you **see and hear at the same time**. Click a wedge and its triad sounds, the wheel outlines the three slices its chords live in, roman numerals appear on those six wedges, the piano lights the notes, and text names them for a reader who cannot hear. Colour is the second channel: Mr Mars' cited scheme, fixed against the theme because it is content, and never the only signal — every wedge is lettered. Neither channel teaches the idea alone: that is the whole point. It also reads in five languages, measures every wedge label against WCAG AA contrast, and follows the reader's light or dark setting — part of the explanation, not extras.

## The moments that mattered

**Happy Birthday highlighted the wrong piano keys.** In some keys it lit keys that were not sounding, and its last line played an octave too low. Two bugs, one hid the other: the melody was pinned to the tonic rather than a fifth below it, pushing 12 of the 24 keys off the keyboard, and `findPianoKey` hid that by falling back to the nearest octave of that note. The obvious fix was a longer keyboard. Instead I re-pinned the melody to its own lowest note and deleted the fallback: a wrong highlight looks exactly like a right one, so lighting nothing is honest. I checked it with a MutationObserver recording each key as it lit, across all 24 keys ([`640421f`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass1-PetreWei/commit/640421f)).

**CI's link check went red because of sites I do not control.** Splitting the theory page took the site from 2 Wikipedia links to 10, and Wikipedia rate-limits a shared CI machine, so it failed only there. The obvious fix was one line in the workflow to skip that host. I drafted it and threw it away: the course CI is the sensor I have to satisfy, and editing it turns a real signal into a green light. I cut the citations back and capped the count in my own tests; when a second site began blocking CI I removed the dependency. Citations keep their full URL as selectable text, but nothing is a link: stricter, not looser — nothing external, anywhere ([`4bd923d..90fdf9d`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass1-PetreWei/compare/4bd923d...90fdf9d)).

**The header tabs and the chord table overflowed at 390px.** The five tabs wrapped onto two rows: French needs about 460px, a phone gives 326px. I took width back from three places — page padding, tab padding, label size — instead of squeezing one. Verifying that turned up a worse bug already shipped: the Chords table pushed the Spanish and Italian pages sideways, their degree names being far longer than English's — I had checked every language's home page but only the English prose page. The table now sits in a scroll box, the tests assert the structure that makes overflow impossible, and I checked the rendering in a browser at 320, 360 and 390px on all 25 pages ([`3adcd7e`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass1-PetreWei/commit/3adcd7e)).

**Nine of the 24 wedge labels failed WCAG AA contrast.** Asked for accessibility, I measured first. Every label used the same white ink, chosen so a future dark theme would have one value to swap. E major came back at 1.57:1. The obvious fix was to darken those colours, but the sameness *was* the cause, so I reversed my own decision: each wedge now picks white or near-black by measured contrast, worst case 4.61:1. The same audit found `:focus-visible` identical to `:hover`, so a keyboard user saw nothing. 24 contrast checks now run in CI, anchored on the WCAG formula — black on white must be exactly 21:1 — so they cannot pass on a broken one ([`c8ade29`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass1-PetreWei/commit/c8ade29)).
